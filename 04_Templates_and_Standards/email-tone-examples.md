# Email Tone Examples

These three examples show how I write professional emails. Use them to match
my voice when drafting any external or internal correspondence on my behalf.

---

## Example 1 — Internal Update to a Senior Peer (Neutral, Informational)

**To:** Marcus Lim
**Subject:** Q3 Staffing Scenarios — Draft Ready for Your Review

Marcus,

The Q3 utilization model is ready. Tom and I have run three scenarios based
on the August bench concentration and the Austin hiring gaps.

Short version: Scenario B gives us the best margin outcome while keeping UR
above 74%. Scenario A is more aggressive on placement but carries real risk if
the London scope expansion doesn't close.

The full model is in SharePoint (link below). I'd like 30 minutes Thursday
to walk through it before we present to Diana on Friday.

Does 10:00 AM CT work?

— Alex

[SharePoint link]

---

> **What to notice:** Short paragraphs. Bottom line up front. One ask, clearly
> stated. No filler opening ("Hope you're doing well"). Signed with first name only.

## Example 2 — External Email to a Vendor (Firm, Professional)

**To:** Sarah Mendenhall, Account Executive, Ramp
**Subject:** RE: Platform Evaluation — Next Steps

Sarah,

Thank you for the demo on Tuesday. We appreciated the depth of the product
walkthrough.

We are currently evaluating three platforms and expect to make a decision by
August 15. Before then, we need two things from your team:

1. A formal pricing proposal for 260 seats, including implementation costs
   and Year 1 support
2. References from two clients in professional services with 200–300 employees

Please send both by July 25. If that timeline is a problem, let me know now
so we can adjust our process accordingly.

Best regards,
Alex Rivera
Director of Operations, Northpoint Group

---

> **What to notice:** Cordial but no small talk. Numbered asks with a hard deadline.
> "Let me know now" — I move fast and expect vendors to match pace. Full
> professional signature on external emails.

## Example 3 — Difficult Internal Email (Sensitive, Diplomatic but Direct)

**To:** Kenji Yamamoto
**Subject:** Facilities Budget — We Need to Talk Before Friday

Kenji,

I've reviewed the Q2 facilities actuals and the variance is larger than I
expected — we're 18% over in the Chicago office alone.

I know some of this is the HVAC repairs that came in over estimate, and I'm
not questioning your judgment on approving those. What I need to understand
before the Friday report is:

- What portion of the overage was unforeseeable vs. avoidable?
- Are there commitments in H2 that we should be aware of now?

Can you send me a brief written summary by Thursday morning? I want to review
it before we discuss rather than hearing it cold on Friday.

— Alex

---

> **What to notice:** Leads with the concern, not with reassurance or small talk.
> Acknowledges what's not being criticized before asking for what is needed.
> Requests written summary before the meeting — this is a consistent pattern
> in how I handle sensitive issues. Tone is serious but not accusatory.
